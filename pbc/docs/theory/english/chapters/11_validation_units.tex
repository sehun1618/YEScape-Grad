\section{Numerical validation, convergence, and reporting units}
\label{sec:validation}

\subsection{Full-energy central finite differences}

The authoritative numerical reference for an analytic gradient is
\begin{equation}
  g_{Ax}^{\mathrm{FD}}(h)
  =\frac{E_{\mathrm{DC-DFA}}(\vR+h\mathbf e_{Ax})
         -E_{\mathrm{DC-DFA}}(\vR-h\mathbf e_{Ax})}{2h}.
  \label{eq:central-fd}
\end{equation}
At each displaced geometry, the source HF problem is reconverged with the same
tolerances and the target-DFA energy is evaluated anew.  Holding the central
density or MOs fixed while differentiating only an operator is a component
diagnostic, not Eq.~\eqref{eq:central-fd}.

The leading truncation error is $O(h^2)$.  If $h$ is too small, SCF and
floating-point noise are amplified as $O(\epsilon/h)$.  One should locate a
plateau over several steps $h$, $h/2$, and $h/4$, and verify that the analytic
gradient agrees with that plateau.

\subsection{Error norms}

For a selected component set $\mathcal I$, record
\begin{align}
  \Delta_{\max}
  &=\max_{(A,x)\in\mathcal I}|g_{Ax}-g_{Ax}^{\mathrm{FD}}|,
  \label{eq:fd-max-error}\\
  \Delta_{\mathrm{RMS}}
  &=\sqrt{\frac1{|\mathcal I|}\sum_{(A,x)\in\mathcal I}
          |g_{Ax}-g_{Ax}^{\mathrm{FD}}|^2}.
  \label{eq:fd-rms-error}
\end{align}
A calculation that agrees for only one axis or one atom is not a full-gradient
validation.  A completion gate includes all $3N_{\mathrm{atom}}$ components of
a distorted geometry.

\subsection{SCF and Z-vector residuals}

The energy and density tolerances of each displaced HF calculation must be
tighter than the target gradient accuracy.  The HF orbital residual, density
change, and energy change are recorded together.  For the Z-vector, record the
maximum residual of Eq.~\eqref{eq:zvector-residual} and a condition estimate.
Even if CPU and GPU produce the same gradient, parity is not established if
both rely on cancellation with a large residual.

\subsection{Convergence of the representation}

After analytic--FD agreement is established, converge one numerical parameter
at a time:
\begin{itemize}
  \item primary Gaussian basis and GTH pseudopotential,
  \item FFTDF cutoff and resolved integer mesh,
  \item target-XC integration mesh,
  \item GDF/RSDF auxiliary basis,
  \item GDF screening and reciprocal mesh,
  \item RSDF source $\omega_B$/mesh and metric $\omega_M$/mesh, and
  \item k-point mesh.
\end{itemize}

Do not report a cutoff without its resolved mesh.  The same cutoff gives a
different integer mesh when the lattice length changes.  RSDF metadata must
not collapse source and metric parameters into a single \texttt{omega} or
\texttt{mesh}.

\subsection{Symmetry-independent checks}

Define the translational sum-rule error as
\begin{equation}
  \Delta_{\mathrm{trans}}
  =\left\|\sum_A\mathbf g_A\right\|_2.
  \label{eq:translation-error}
\end{equation}
With only symmetric H$_2$, missing terms can cancel by symmetry.  A mandatory
test therefore uses H$_2$O with slightly distorted bonds, angle, and overall
orientation.  Both Gamma and sampled k-points, and both real and complex
tensor paths, are included.

Equivalent Gamma representations must satisfy
\begin{equation}
  E_{\Gamma}=E_{N_k=1},
  \qquad
  \mathbf g_{\Gamma}=\mathbf g_{N_k=1}.
  \label{eq:gamma-one-kpoint-parity}
\end{equation}
This catches shape-adapter errors that incorrectly remove or retain a
singleton k-axis.

\subsection{CPU/GPU parity}

For identical cells, bases, pseudopotentials, meshes, k-points, and SCF
tolerances, record
\begin{align}
  \Delta_E^{\mathrm{CPU/GPU}}
  &=|E_{\mathrm{CPU}}-E_{\mathrm{GPU}}|,
  \label{eq:cpu-gpu-energy-error}\\
  \Delta_g^{\mathrm{CPU/GPU}}
  &=\max_{Ax}|g_{Ax}^{\mathrm{CPU}}-g_{Ax}^{\mathrm{GPU}}|.
  \label{eq:cpu-gpu-gradient-error}
\end{align}
GDF/RSDF integral generation may be an explicit GPU host boundary.  A GPU path
that simply calls the CPU package's final gradient and returns that value is
not parity; it is fallback.  Ownership is verified through capability and
timing metadata.

\subsection{Initial acceptance thresholds}

The initial completion targets for this project are
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{ll}
\toprule
Test & Threshold \\
\midrule
Analytic vs converged full-energy FD & $\Delta_{\max}\le10^{-5}$ Ha/Bohr \\
CPU/GPU energy & $\Delta_E\le10^{-8}$ Ha \\
CPU/GPU gradient & $\Delta_g\le10^{-7}$ Ha/Bohr \\
Translation norm & $\Delta_{\mathrm{trans}}\le10^{-6}$ Ha/Bohr \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Thresholds are interpreted together with basis, cutoff, and FD-step
convergence evidence.  Exact analytic--FD agreement on a coarse mesh does not
imply that the resulting gradient is the converged physical gradient.

\subsection{Result metadata}

An energy/gradient result preserves at least the following information in a
machine-readable form:
\begin{itemize}
  \item method, target XC, source reference, charge, and spin,
  \item atomic coordinates, lattice, and the unit of each,
  \item basis and pseudopotential,
  \item Gamma/k-point list and weights,
  \item DF backend and auxiliary basis,
  \item cutoff and resolved FFT/XC meshes,
  \item RSDF source and metric settings,
  \item HF convergence and energy timings,
  \item Z dimension, residual, condition estimate, and gradient-component
        timings,
  \item device, host boundaries, and a silent-fallback flag, and
  \item gradient-array unit and the force-sign convention.
\end{itemize}

The canonical gradient output is a NumPy array in Ha/Bohr.  Rather than
duplicate large arrays in JSON, metadata may record the shape, dtype, unit,
checksum, and path to an NPY file.
